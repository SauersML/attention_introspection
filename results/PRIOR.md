# Prior work: instructed attention, attention self-report, attention intervention → self-report
Searched 18 Nov 2026 (Exa, DuckDuckGo, arXiv API, LessWrong). Only claims I read in the paper text (PDF or abstract/page) are stated. PDFs/text in compute/attn_intro/lit/.

## Our three results
1. "While you answer, focus your attention on [P3]" raises answer-token→P3 attention ~+27% vs a matched control that only mentions P3, answer text unchanged; "do not pay attention to" lowers it ~10% (32B); bigger at 32B; concentrated in layers 21+ of 64 (Qwen3-8B/32B).
2. Asked which paragraph it attended to most, the model is no better than the same model reading the transcript.
3. Boosting attention logits on a paragraph (answer identical) moves the self-report log-prob for that paragraph DOWN; transcript reader unchanged.

## (a) Instructions controlling where attention goes, measured in the attention pattern
- **Zhang, Meng, Collier, "Attention Instruction: Amplifying Attention in the Middle via Prompting"**, arXiv 2406.17095 = EMNLP Findings 2024 ("Can We Instruct LLMs to Compensate for Position Bias?", https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.732/). Adds "attention instructions" naming a document ID to multi-doc QA prompts; when told to focus on a document by ID, "the average attention score of the tokens in that document increases, regardless of the gold document position"; relative-position instructions ("middle part") fail; Mistral-7B heatmaps; "front layers are more sensitive". Comparison is instruction vs no instruction (difference heatmaps), and the instruction also tells the model where the answer is, so the answer changes (accuracy is the main metric). → **Shows the core of (1)** (prompted focus raises measured attention to the named span). Does NOT have: mention-matched control, answer held fixed, "do not attend" direction on attention, scale trend, late-layer locus (they report the opposite: early layers).
- **Zhang, Meng, Collier, "Failure Modes in Multi-Hop QA: The Weakest Link Effect and the Recognition Bottleneck"**, arXiv 2601.12499. Multi-Focus Attention Instruction (template "The answer is in Document X and …"); Fig. 5: attention-mass difference (Matched − No MFAI) for Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct shifts toward gold documents, "uniform across heads but concentrated in deep layers"; averaged over examples where MFAI rescued the answer. → **Closest replication of (1) incl. deep-layer locus.** Control is no-instruction, the instruction is informational ("answer is in X"), answers differ; no "ignore" arm on attention; attention shown at 7B only.
- **Ho et al., "Language Models Can Control Their Own Attention" (Declarative Attention)**, arXiv 2609.02737. Model declares in its CoT which chunk it will focus on; the engine enforces a hard attention mask. Measures attended-token counts and accuracy; does not test whether the model's natural attention follows its declaration without the mask (DA_nm runs the prompt unmasked but reports cost/accuracy only, per §5.1). → Not (1); the title overlaps ours, so cite to distinguish.
- **GazeVLM (Ebouky et al.)**, arXiv 2605.07817. VLM trained (SFT+GRPO) with an attention-logit bias tied to <LOOK> boxes; with bias removed, attention IoU with the named box 0.36 vs base 0.13. → (1)-like but trained, vision, self-declared; not zero-shot instruction.
- **Lindsey, "Emergent Introspective Awareness in LLMs"** (transformer-circuits.pub/2025/introspection, arXiv 2601.01828). "Intentional control": "think about"/"don't think about" a word raises the word's activation-vector alignment; "less strongly (though still above baseline) when instructed not to think about it". → Activation analogue of (1), not attention. Note the contrast: their "don't" is still above baseline; our "do not attend" goes below the mention control.
- **Ji-An, Xiong, Wilson, "LLMs Are Capable of Metacognitive Monitoring and Control of Their Internal Activations"**, arXiv 2505.13763. Neurofeedback: report/control activation directions in context. → Activations, not attention.
- Method side (external reweighting, not instruction): PASTA 2311.02262; AutoPASTA 2409.10790; SpotLight (EACL 2026, aclanthology 2026.eacl-long.174); InstABoost 2506.13734; Focus directions 2503.23306 (learned Q/K offsets raise attention to relevant context). → Tools for (3)-style interventions; none ask for a self-report.
- Shi et al. 2302.00093 (GSM-IC): "ignore irrelevant information" instruction helps accuracy; no attention measurement.

## (b) Self-report of attention vs actual attention
- I found **no paper** that asks a model which part it attended to and scores that against its measured attention weights. Nearest:
  - **Singh, Linzen, Ravfogel, "Can LLMs Introspect? A Reality Check"**, arXiv 2605.26242: classifiers with input access only match models' in-context predictions of labels derived from their hidden states → no privileged access. Same logic as our transcript-reader null; different object (hidden-state labels).
  - **Binder et al., "Looking Inward"**, arXiv 2410.13787: self-prediction vs cross-prediction (M1 vs M2 predicting M1's behaviour) after finetuning. Behaviour, not attention.
  - **Naphade et al., "Me, Myself, and π"**, arXiv 2603.20276: frontier models beat peers at predicting own behaviour; mechanism "attention diffusion". Behaviour, not attention reports.
  - Self-explanation faithfulness (feature attribution / important words): Huang et al. 2310.11207; Madsen et al. ACL Findings 2024 (aclanthology 2024.findings-acl.19); Fayyaz et al. 2407.00219 (prompted rationales less faithful than attention/gradient attributions). Compare self-reported importance to causal/attribution measures, not to the model's own attention, and without a transcript-reader control.
  - **Zhou et al., "Did the Model Use That?"**, arXiv 2610.35295: models rarely self-attribute influential supplied info; counterfactual training fixes it. Influence, not attention.
- → **(2) is a new object (attention) for a known null (no privileged access; Singh et al. is the closest).**

## (c) Intervene on attention / KV, check the self-report
- **"A Mechanistic Study of Language Model Introspection"** (HKU), arXiv 2609.35108. In a concept-injection localisation task, redirecting the attention of late "router heads" at the report position to another candidate changes the reported position toward the redirected one. → Attention intervention moves a self-report, but the intervention is on the heads that compute the report, the report is about an injected vector, and the shift is in the intended direction. Not (3).
- **Afendulev et al., "Context or Knowledge: How LLMs Attribute Their Own Answers"**, arXiv 2610.32109. Edits the KV cache after answering: changing the document cache never changes the "context vs knowledge" source report; transplanting the Q+A cache transfers it; value-direction steering flips reports. Notes that additive key edits caused nonspecific report changes even for random directions. → Closest to (3) in design (intervene on internal state with answer fixed, read the source report), but on KV values/keys, report is binary source, and reports follow the cached state.
- "Detecting the Disturbance" 2512.12411, OWMI 2608.20569, Rubin 2611.15267, Lindsey: interventions on activations/heads with self-report; none boost attention to a context span and check an "attended-to" report.
- → **(3) appears new.** I found no work that steers attention logits toward a span during the answer and measures the model's report of where it attended, nor any reporting an inverted report shift.

## Conclusion
- (1) **Partial replication.** Prompted focus raising measured attention to a named document is shown by Zhang, Meng, Collier (2406.17095, EMNLP Findings 2024) and their MFAI follow-up (2601.12499, deep-layer concentration in Qwen2.5-7B). New in ours: mention-matched control with the answer held fixed (their instructions also say where the answer is and change the answer), the "do not attend" arm lowering attention, the 8B→32B increase. Activation analogue: Lindsey 2025 "think/don't think".
- (2) **New on attention, known as a pattern.** No prior attention self-report test found; the same-model transcript-reader null matches Singh/Linzen/Ravfogel 2605.26242 (input-only classifiers match self-reports) and the Binder 2410.13787 cross-prediction design.
- (3) **New.** Closest: 2609.35108 (redirecting report-time head attention moves the report, same direction) and 2610.32109 (KV-cache edits move source reports). Neither boosts attention to content during answering, and neither finds an inverted shift.
Caveats: searches can miss workshop papers and recent posts; I did not find a LessWrong/AF post on attention self-report. I did not read 2609.35108's appendix in full, only the abstract and method text.
